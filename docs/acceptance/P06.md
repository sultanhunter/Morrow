Notes:
- **AC09 wording.** The packet lists a foreign organization among the cases that "fail with 400 before SQL". The organization is not part of the request: it is known only once `enter_context` has resolved the session, so this one case opens a connection and is refused (400) after the context and before the command function. Every other AC09 case is 400 with no SQL connection opened. Resolved 2026-10-18 as a routine packet-wording correction: the packet's AC09 text is amended to match.
- **AC14 wording.** The packet says "C cancelling A's question → 403", but its fixture gives C no question grants, and §4.7 step 4 makes a record the actor cannot view a 404 identical to an absent one. The implementation follows §4.7: C gets 404; a principal who can view the question but is neither its requester nor an administrator gets 403. Resolved 2026-10-18 as a routine packet-wording correction consistent with §4.7 (not-found indistinguishability).
- **Residual risk (ADR-0010 item 2).** A compromised api Worker holding a live victim session could exceed that victim's grants within the victim's workspace; it cannot cross tenants or outlive a revocation.
- **Resolver cost.** Every row-level-security evaluation re-resolves the tenant through `session_eligibility()`, about 14 times per command, and PostgreSQL 17 plans a SQL function body on every call. The two resolver bridges pin the written join order (`join_collapse_limit`/`from_collapse_limit` = 1), which cut a command from about 600 ms to about 150 ms on the 1-CPU laboratory engine without changing results. Four concurrent writers to one workspace serialize on its stream lock with `lock_timeout` 1 s, so a slow database turns contention into 503s that a same-key retry resolves.
- **Organization interlock.** A row `FOR SHARE` alone was not fair: PostgreSQL grants a new share lock past a waiting `FOR UPDATE`, so overlapping commands could keep a revocation waiting past its 5 s statement timeout. Commands now take a heavyweight shared advisory lock on the organization first, and the identity writers that lock an organization `FOR UPDATE` take it exclusively first; new shared requests queue behind a waiting exclusive one.
- **Not qualified here.** The Neon WebSocket transport against a hosted database, Hyperdrive or any pooler, and the PG-wire labs on Linux; the hosted migration of 0014–0015 is a separate owner-approved action.
