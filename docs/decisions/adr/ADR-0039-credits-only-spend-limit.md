## Context

Spend has been bounded by a stack of money caps. ADR-0022 item 11 (2026-10-19) set US$7 of AI cost per run and US$25 per workspace per day, and limits a Listings run to the lower of 25 products and US$7 over the selection's worst case. ADR-0026 item 4 kept them as USD backstops and added rate card v1 caps: per installation a day and a month, per workspace a day, a look-for-work cap and the specialists' 5% of the monthly grant per run and 20% per day. ADR-0027 items 6 and 12 derive each loop run's turn limit and hold from US$7 and those caps. ADR-0036 removed the unrecovered-cost cap, set the look-for-work cap to US$1.12 per agent per day and kept US$7 and US$25 at the owner's answer. The values the lead first listed to carry out ADR-0037 (pull request 72) would have carried the specialist caps over to teammates Morrow creates, beside US$7 and US$25; ADR-0037, as the owner then settled it, sets no money cap of its own (its item 6).

In code on `main`, Listings' `run_reserve` refuses `daily_cap` and the product limit, and the runtime refuses a sandbox past a US$10 monthly budget. The agent loop (pull request 64, switch off) holds the lowest of its turn limit times its worst-case turn and US$7, refuses at the daily backstop, and before the rate card holds no credits at all (ADR-0027 item 6). Taking the money caps away without more would leave loop runs with no limit, so this record also makes them hold credits.

The lead listed the values written to carry out ADR-0037, among them a created teammate's budget of 5% and 20% beside US$7 and US$25. The owner answered: "for budget why we limiting anything? it should be very simple credit based system" (OD-2026-10-25-4, quoted in full under Approval). The lead replied with the reading below.

## Decision

1. **Credits are the only spend limit.** These go, for every run kind and every agent of either origin: US$7 per run; the Listings product limit derived from it; US$25 per workspace per day; the look-for-work cap of US$1.12 per agent per day; the specialist and created-teammate caps of 5% of the monthly grant per run and 20% per workspace per day; the rate card v1 caps per installation (a day and a month) and per workspace (a day); RateCard@1's `backstops`; a mandate's credit caps (MandateCaps); the runtime's US$10 monthly budget (item 6). The unrecovered-cost cap, which ADR-0036 removed, stays removed. Published records and catalogue versions keep the values they were approved with; nothing enforces them once the code lands.
2. **Each step holds and draws credits from the workspace's wallet** (`morrow_billing.credit_wallets`), in SQL under the wallet's row lock, never in a prompt.
   - (a) **Paid steps:** a loop run's model turn, a Listings product, a computer session, and an email send once the rate card prices it. ADR-0026 item 5's free list stays free.
   - (b) **Hold before the step.** A step holds its worst case in whole credits before it runs. Every hold on a wallet takes its row lock, so the open holds of all jobs together, teammates called in parallel included, never exceed the balance.
   - (c) **Draw after the step.** Its metered use is booked at the run's pinned rates (item 3). The run's hold then drops to its use so far, rounded up to a whole credit, plus its final-turn reserve. A run settles once, at its end, in whole credits, never above what it held, and releases the rest (ADR-0026 item 5's once-per-run conversion).
   - (d) **Admission.** A loop run first holds its final-turn reserve (proposed, pending the owner's answer, Q-adr-0039-carried-out-values): one worst-case turn of its entry, kept for the run's life so that it can always finish with an answer. A run that must call a tool (a mandate run, look-for-work included) also needs one more worst-case turn. If the wallet cannot cover that, admission returns `insufficient_credits`: a person's run is refused ("Not enough credits to start this job"); a proactive trigger stays pending until the balance covers it, never dropped (ADR-0024 item 6); a teammate call returns the code to Morrow at once. ADR-0026 item 3(e)'s wait for an unconfirmed block stays, at admission only.
   - (e) **Running out.** Before each non-final turn the run holds that turn's worst case. If the wallet cannot cover it, the run takes its final turn from its reserve, with no tools (ADR-0027 item 4), and ends `credits_exhausted`: "Stopped: the workspace ran out of credits. Add credits to continue." A run never waits for credits mid-run.
   - (f) **Uncertain outcomes.** An uncertain turn stays drawn at its worst case: never promoted to free, never lowered without a provider record that proves less. Its one resend (ADR-0027 item 4) needs a new worst-case hold before it is sent; without one the run ends `credits_exhausted` and does not resend. Listings' uncertain attempts are booked the same way (ADR-0022 item 11). Reconciliation before any retry stays.
   - (g) **However the run ends** (proposed, pending the owner's answer, Q-adr-0039-carried-out-values). A loop run's turns draw their metered credits whatever the run's end; a turn with no metered use (refused before the send, a provider error with no usage, the provider's spend limit) draws nothing. This would replace ADR-0026 item 4's owner-approved "Failed runs cost 0 credits" for loop runs, a price change that neither the owner nor the lead's reply decided. Until the owner answers, it serves only the test credits on the owner's store (item 3), "Failed runs cost 0 credits" stands for merchants, and rate card v1 does not publish before the answer. Listings keeps its published price: a failed product is not charged (ADR-0022 item 11), and the breaker stops a run after three failures in a row.
3. **Prices, and no free running.** A step's credits come from the rate card (ADR-0026 item 5): for each catalogue entry, credits per million tokens of each kind and a worst-case turn in whole credits; computer seconds and email sends at their own rates. A run pins its rate card version at admission beside its catalogue version. Admission refuses an entry the pinned rate card does not price (`entry_unpriced`). Rate card v1 publishes only with the owner's recorded confirmation (ADR-0026 item 5). Until then Morrow runs only on the owner's store on test credits (the operator test entitlement and ADR-0026 item 7's seed, ADR-0035 item 3), and loop runs draw them at rate card v1's proposed values, which the code PR writes by the margin rule. No loop run runs free, and no merchant pays before publication.
4. **Listings.** A `listings-run@1` run keeps its up-front hold of products times credits per product (ADR-0022 item 11), so it never runs out mid-run. Its product limit is the version's `max_products_per_run`, 25, for every entry, no longer lowered by US$7 over the worst case (Opus 5.5 at high goes from 9 to 25 products, at medium from 12 to 25, Sonnet 5.5 at high from 19 to 25). `run_reserve` no longer refuses `daily_cap`. A published version never changes (ADR-0022 item 16), so catalogue version 4 publishes on this decision: version 3 with no run or daily cap and a product limit of 25 for every entry. Version 3 retires; a run recorded at version 1 or 3 is still booked at its version.
5. **Stop conditions stay, and they are not money.** Per turn: 64,000 input tokens and the effort's `max_tokens`. Per run: the turn limit (16, and 6 for Morrow), 4 tool calls a turn and 40 a run, 2 questions, 72 hours unanswered, and 30 minutes of wall time with waits excluded. Listings: 25 products and 2 attempts a product. Proactive work: one look-for-work pass per agent a day, one run per trigger key, and the running limit. The computer: 10 sandboxes at once, 3 per workspace, and each session's deadline (ADR-0029 item 8). They are catalogue and runtime data, changed by a new version, and no money value lowers them: the turn limit is the version's, no longer lowered so that it times the worst-case turn fits US$7. Their values are catalogue version 2's, which the owner confirms with its publication (Q-catalogue-v2-values); ADR-0037 may set Morrow's own turn limit for orchestration. Any person who holds a stop can end any job at its next step (ADR-0005).
6. **Outside backstops.** The owner's Anthropic monthly spend limit is US$100, set by the owner in the owner's Anthropic account (as the lead stated in reply, OD-2026-10-25-4; the owner's own statement of the amount is quoted in pull request 73's brief). A call it refuses is `spend_limit` (ADR-0022 item 10): no retry, nothing drawn, the run ends with "Stopped: the AI provider's spending limit is reached", and the owner is told. E2B's usage limit in the owner's E2B account does the same for computer time, so the runtime's US$10 monthly budget below it goes (ADR-0029 item 8), and E2B's refusal sets the runtime hold that only a person lifts (ADR-0029 item 9). Neither limit is in Morrow's code; raising either is the owner's choice.
7. **Settings stay settings.** The Owner's controls are settings, not caps in code: each agent's model and effort (ADR-0027 item 12), the member credit ceiling (ADR-0036 item 2), the extra-credit cap (`UsageCap`: 0 by default, raised only by the Owner, ADR-0026 item 4), pausing an agent or its triggers, and "Let Morrow suggest new teammates". Read from the owner's answer and not yet confirmed by the owner (Q-adr-0039-carried-out-values): the per-installation daily and monthly caps that ADR-0026 item 4 let merchants lower and only the Owner raise go with the other caps, and no per-agent or per-workspace credit limit exists by default. The alternative put to the owner is an optional per-agent credit limit that the Owner sets, none by default. The code PRs remove the hardcoded caps either way; the answer decides only whether that setting is built.
8. **Teammates and orchestration.** A teammate's run, called or handed off, of either origin, holds and draws its own credits from the same wallet like any run. Morrow's run never holds credits for its children. However Morrow orchestrates (many calls, in sequence or in parallel, the same teammate more than once), each run's steps hold under the wallet's lock, so together they never spend past the balance.
9. **Codes.** New: `insufficient_credits` for loop admission (Listings already has it), `credits_exhausted` for a run's end, and `entry_unpriced`. No longer returned, and kept in the closed enums only for records that already carry them: `daily_cap`, `daily_backstop`, `cap_below_turn`, the hold bindings `run_backstop` and `credit_cap`, and `monthly_budget`, as ADR-0036 kept `unrecovered_cost_cap`. `cost_budget` stays for a step whose booked cost passes its own hold (overflow), which still ends the run.

## Security invariants touched

None is weakened. Permission and budget checks still happen in code, outside model prompts; the budget is now the wallet.
- **Budget checks in code:** holds, draws, the final-turn reserve and `insufficient_credits` under the wallet's row lock (`lab:run-credits`, `lab:listings-runs`, `tests/run-credits.test.mjs`, `tests/loop-catalogue.test.mjs`, and pull request 64's `lab:agent-loop`), including two runs racing for the last credits and parallel teammate calls.
- **Unknown outcomes reconciled, never promoted:** item 2(f) (pull request 64's `lab:agent-loop` and `tests/broker-agent-turn.test.mjs`; `lab:run-credits` for Listings).
- **Default deny:** no wallet, entitlement or rate, no run (`entry_unpriced`, `insufficient_credits`; `verify:billing`).
- **Stops:** free, `human_only`, never blocked by credits (`verify:billing` PW2, `stops.test.mjs`).
- **Immutable migrations:** catalogue version 4 and the revised helpers come in forward migrations (`migration-history.test.mjs`).
- **What is removed is a second layer, not an invariant.** The USD caps also bounded the damage of a bug in the credit checks. The outside backstops (item 6) now bound that, and the labs above test the holds directly.

## Consequences

- **What bounds AI cost.** A workspace's AI cost is bounded by its credits, and each credit's price covers its vendor cost by the margin rule: at most 50% of a credit's net revenue for loop work (ADR-0026 item 5), at most 90% for Listings (ADR-0022 item 11). The exceptions are Listings' failed products, which are free and bounded per run by the breaker and 25 products, and steps with no metered use. Above that, the Anthropic US$100 a month bounds every workspace together; in v1 that is the owner's own.
- **Bigger single runs.** A Listings run at Opus 5.5 high may draft 25 products: 150 credits, US$17.60 of worst-case vendor cost (was 9 products, US$6.34). An installed agent at Opus 5.5 medium gets its full 16 turns, US$8.96 at worst (was 12, US$6.72). Morrow keeps 6 turns (US$3.84) until ADR-0037 sets its turn limit for orchestration.
- **Proactive bursts.** With the per-installation daily caps gone, a burst of triggers can spend the balance quickly. Its bounds are the wallet, one run per trigger key, one look-for-work pass a day and the running limit. If staging shows bursts, the remedy is a count limit on proactive runs, not money, by its own decision.
- **On the owner's store** (ADR-0035), the operator test entitlement's 300 test credits are the only limit on AI work, beside the Anthropic US$100. Raising it, to 1,000 credits for example, is a new staging BillingTerms revision.
- **An illustration only.** If a credit carried the beta terms (US$49 for 300 credits, a 17.9% fee, the 50% vendor share), it would hold about US$0.067 of model cost. A worst-case Morrow turn at Opus 5.5 high (US$0.64) would hold 10 credits, and an expected Ask (US$0.46) would draw 7. So 1,000 credits would cover about 140 Asks. The real values come with rate card v1.
- **Written to carry out the decision, and put to the owner** (Q-adr-0039-carried-out-values), to be answered before rate card v1 publishes (ADR-0026 item 5): the final-turn reserve (item 2(d) and (e)), loop turns drawing credits however the run ends (item 2(g)), the platform ceiling on buying credits kept ("Kept, and why" below) and no per-agent credit limit (item 7). ## Alternatives considered

- **Keep US$7 and US$25 as hidden safety nets.** The owner rejected hardcoded caps; the Anthropic limit is the outside net.
- **Hold a loop run's whole worst case at admission,** as Listings does. With no run cap that is the turn limit times the worst-case turn, for example 16 turns of 10 credits, which locks credits a typical run never uses and refuses runs that would fit. Holding one step at a time does not.
- **Default per-agent credit limits.** The owner asked for no hardcoded limits. An optional limit that the Owner sets, none by default, is put to the owner (Q-adr-0039-carried-out-values).
- **Charging Listings' failed products.** A price change nobody asked for.

## Approval

The owner's statement in chat, verbatim, on 2026-10-25, answering the lead's list of the values written to carry out ADR-0037, among them a created teammate's budget of 5% of the monthly credits per job and 20% per day plus US$7 per job and US$25 per day (OD-2026-10-25-4):

> for budget why we limiting anything? it should be very simple credit based system like suppose we give user 1000credits it will be spend on the agentic work so that creidt will bcome limit itself no need to hardcode all of that bullshit. and for one i want it to be truty multi agent system so the agents can be orchestrated by the main agent in any way no need to limit to a single agent call only. for 2 why we adding limit on characters? it might strip important information i think only needs to be structured. for other option use the recommded one

What it approves: credits as the only spend limit, with every fixed money cap removed (item 1), as the lead's reply to the owner stated it: each job reserves and draws credits from the workspace balance as it works and stops when the balance cannot cover the next step; the Anthropic monthly limit stays as the outside backstop; non-money stop conditions stay; the Owner's settings stay settings.

Written from it, to carry it out: item 2's holds, draws and uncertain-outcome rule; item 3's rule that no loop run runs free; item 4's catalogue version 4; item 6's removal of the runtime budget, under the reply's "any other hardcoded money ceiling"; item 9's codes; and the inventory under Consequences.

Not approved by it, and put to the owner (Q-adr-0039-carried-out-values): the final-turn reserve (item 2(d) and (e)), the draw of a failed loop run's turns (item 2(g)), keeping the platform ceiling on buying credits (Consequences, "Kept, and why") and having no per-agent credit limit (item 7).
