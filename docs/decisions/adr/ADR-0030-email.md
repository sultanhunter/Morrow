# ADR-0030 Email: inbound support mail by forwarding, approved replies through Resend, template-only notifications

## Context

The owner approved decision 8 with its recommended vendor: "yes to all recommended" (Q12). `main` has no mail code; `support.reply.send` is catalogued as restricted, review-required and irreversible; and the 0020 operations ledger never turns `uncertain` into success. Items 1, 2, 5 and 9 follow the public documentation of [Resend](https://resend.com/docs) (24-hour idempotency keys, a message read API, send-only keys) and [Cloudflare Email Routing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/email-routing/), read 2026-10-23.

## Decision

1. **Inbound addresses.** Each workspace has one address, `inbox+<random token>@in.<domain>`, routed by one Email Routing rule per environment to an `email` handler in the connector Worker. A definer finds the workspace from the token in the envelope recipient only; headers never select a tenant. Unknown or rotated addresses are rejected; uninstalling Support closes the address. Gmail's forwarding code is shown only to the person setting it up.
2. **Inbound mail is untrusted data.** Email Routing's rejection is the launch sender check (owner question 4). A pinned parser keeps fixed headers and up to 64 KiB of plain text; raw MIME and attachments are never stored. Mail is `customer_mail` under the `support.case.read` tag. No run starts for auto-replies, bounces, list mail, Morrow's own copies, or mail past 300 a day per workspace.
3. **Drafting a reply.** Only Support's package may declare `support.*` capabilities; CI and install refuse any other manifest that does, and the Coordinator and specialists hold none. Support's tools take a thread id and a body; code sets every header, From from the job's sender identity (item 10). **To** is the inbound From address only; a message without exactly one starts no run. Order and customer reads match that address exactly, so order facts go only to the email on the order. Refunds and cancellations go to a person. Every reply carries `contextTaint: untrusted_external` and ADR-0011 Q3's communication rules.
4. **The send.** `support.reply.send` runs only at the gateway, after approval of its exact digest over the sender identity id, From, To, Bcc, Reply-To, Subject, In-Reply-To, References and the plain-text body; any edit voids the approval. The broker's `mailSend(operationId)` takes only the id, needs a live lease and no stop, re-checks the sender identity (item 10(e)), and treats a changed sender, From, Reply-To or Bcc as a `conflict`. One OperationLedger operation has one key for life, `morrow-mail:<environment>:<operationId>`, and makes one `POST /emails`. Replies are plain text with no attachments or tracking; code refuses CR and LF in headers. The broker marks the send started under a row lock before calling Resend.
5. **Unknown outcomes: reconcile, never retry blind.** A 200 with an id is `submitted`; a documented pre-send refusal is `failed`; a 429 gets one same-key resend; anything else after the mark is `uncertain`.
   - **(a)** With a message id, Morrow reads its status; the read needs no lease and no stop check.
   - **(b)** Without one, at most two same-key replays, only while the lease is live, no stop applies and authority re-checks; then (a).
   - **(c)** Otherwise the operation stays `uncertain` and an Inbox item asks the approver and the Owner to check the Bcc copy. "Send again" is a new proposal, digest, approval and key, and its card warns that the first message may have arrived.
6. **Verification.** An operation is `verified` only when the status read matches the approved headers, `html` is empty, and the text's SHA-256 equals the approved body digest. Delivery status is polled for 72 hours and shown in Activity; a bounce raises an Inbox item.
7. **Bcc and identities.** Every reply from the Morrow address has exactly one Bcc and Reply-To: the workspace's support address, verified by a 6-digit code, changed only by the Owner through `governance.mail_identity.revise` (`human_only`, audited; item 10). Replies come from the sender identity the job carries (item 10), at launch the shop's Morrow address, `<handle>@replies.<domain>` with the shop's name (owner question 1, as changed); notifications come from `notify.<domain>` and are never on the list. Nothing is sent from an unverified domain.
8. **Notifications are fixed templates.** Templates live in the repository with closed variables: counts, kinds, dates, the workspace name and Morrow links; never model output, mail, product text, names, amounts or customer data. Recipients are members' verified emails, each built from what that member may see. They are system sends with no approval and no credits. Each send is a row in a new content-free `mail_deliveries` table (ids, template id, recipient member id, key and status; no OperationLedger operation, so no taskless contract change), reconciled by item 5(a) and (b) with the same key, and never sent with a new key.
9. **Limits, custody and the switch.** The broker accepts only three recipient kinds: the thread's From address, the verified support address as Bcc, and a member's verified email; item 7's 6-digit code, a fixed template, also goes to the support address being verified. It sends only from an `active` sender identity (item 10) or, for notifications, from `notify.<domain>`. No supplier, marketing or bulk mail, and no Gmail or Outlook OAuth at launch: those mailboxes are item 10's reserved kinds, each by its own ADR. It holds `RESEND_REPLY_KEY` (the `morrow_managed` kind's adapter, item 10(f)) and `RESEND_NOTIFY_KEY` (send-only, one domain each) and `RESEND_READ_KEY` (owner question 3), which never leave it. `docs/decisions/mail-staging.json` is committed `"off"`; while off, the handler rejects mail and the broker refuses sends. `support.reply.send` is refused until G10. Staging sends only to test and allowlisted owner addresses, under staging card 2. Defaults (owner question 5): 200 replies and 300 inbound runs per workspace a day; 5 hard bounces or 2 complaints in 7 days set a system stop on Support that only a person lifts.
10. **Sending addresses** (owner question 1 as the owner changed it, OD-2026-10-23-7). Each workspace has a list of addresses it may send from. Every email job sends from the one a person picks, and the shop's Morrow address is on the list and selected by default until the shop links its own address (item 10(c)).
    - **(a) The record.** `sender_identities` (FORCE RLS) holds `SenderIdentity@1`: id, kind, the store it came from, address, display name, status (`active`, `disabled` or `retired`), use scope and revision, with at most one default per workspace (a partial unique index). The kind is a closed enum with one value at launch, `morrow_managed`. `google_mailbox`, `microsoft_mailbox` and `merchant_domain` are reserved names; each enters the enum only by its own ADR, because each adds credential custody (a mailbox token in the broker vault) or DNS verification at the mail provider. A launch record holds no credential.
    - **(b) The Morrow address.** The store-link transaction (ADR-0025 item 2) creates it, `active` and the default: `<handle>@replies.<domain>`. Code builds the handle from the shop's name at link time: folded to ASCII without accents, lower-case, each run of other characters turned into one `-`, with no `-` at either end, at most 40 characters. A name that leaves fewer than 3 characters falls back to the shop's `myshopify.com` name, built the same way. The display name is the shop's name, cleaned in code (printable; no CR, LF, quotes or angle brackets; at most 64 characters). A handle that is a reserved name (RFC 2142 role names and Morrow's own, in a committed list) or held by another live address gets `-` and 4 random letters or digits. `sender_address_registry` holds each live address's keyed digest, computed by the broker, with no tenant, so no two workspaces hold the same address; each digest is deleted with its identity. The address never changes, even when the shop's handle or name changes in Shopify; only the Owner changes the display name. The default is always `active`: the Morrow address may be disabled only while another active identity is the default, and it becomes the default again when none is. It is retired and deleted with the store's data (ADR-0031 item 3).
    - **(c) Who manages the list.** Only the Owner, through new `governance.mail_identity.revise` (control, `human_only`, not review-required, audited; agents may only propose it): set the default, change a display name, disable or enable an identity, and change item 7's support address. Linking and unlinking belong to the linked kinds' ADRs, under the owner's rule that the Morrow address is the default only until the shop links its own address: the first linked kind's ADR either makes a newly linked address the default or asks the Owner at linking, and which of the two is an open owner question for that ADR. One audited transaction makes the change and voids the unclaimed approvals of pending proposals whose sender, From, Reply-To or Bcc it changes; a claimed operation finishes and reconciles. A new default never changes an existing proposal's sender.
    - **(d) The job carries its sender.** Every capability that sends email, at launch only `support.reply.send`, carries `senderIdentityId` in its parameters. Code sets it to the workspace default when the proposal is made; no tool takes a sender, no model context receives the list, and model output never sets one (ADR-0027 item 5). Code builds From from the identity's display name and address. The review card shows "Send from" with the identities the viewer may use; picking another is an edit (ADR-0024 item 8), a new proposal whose digest holds the new sender, and it voids the original's approvals. The embedded home shows the sender and never changes it.
    - **(e) Checks at dispatch.** The gateway at dispatch, and the broker before every send and replay (status reads excepted), check that the identity belongs to the proposal's workspace, is `active` and has an adapter, and that the approver may use it, then rebuild From, Reply-To and Bcc; any difference from the approved digest is a `conflict`, and nothing is sent. Use is checked in code and only narrows; it is never a grant. Use scope `workspace`, the launch value, admits anyone who may approve the job (ADR-0011 Q5's reviewer verbs); `personal` is reserved for linked mailboxes, and their ADR sets who may use one.
    - **(f) One adapter per kind.** The broker maps each kind to one send adapter in code: `morrow_managed` to Resend with `RESEND_REPLY_KEY`, whose only domain is `replies.<domain>`. A kind is admitted only with an adapter that keeps items 4 to 6: one key per operation or another proven guard against a second send, a message read for reconciliation, and verification of the sent headers and body. Adding a kind adds its enum value, adapter, vault kind and linking flow. The list, the picker, the job's field, the digest, the checks and retention stay as they are, so a linked address simply appears on the list.
    - **(g) Reply-To, Bcc and inbound.** For the Morrow address, Reply-To and the one Bcc are item 7's verified support address, so a customer's answer reaches the shop's mailbox and comes back by forwarding (item 1). The reply subdomain takes no mail: a second Email Routing rule sends it to the connector Worker's `email` handler, which rejects it at SMTP with a fixed text naming no shop, before any lookup, and starts no run. `postmaster@` and `abuse@` on the subdomain forward to the operator (G7). A linked kind's ADR sets its own Reply-To, copy and inbound.
    - **(h) Launch scope and shape.** The list serves support replies only; item 9 still refuses supplier, marketing and bulk mail. Notifications (item 8) are system sends from `notify.<domain>`, never on the list. Morrow addresses use the dedicated `replies.` subdomain, not the bare domain, so one merchant's bounces and complaints stay away from Morrow's own mail and from notifications, and the reply key cannot send as either.
    - **(i) Contracts and delivery.** T2 generates, closed and with no runtime eval: `SenderIdentity@1`; the enums `SenderIdentityKind`, `SenderIdentityStatus` and `SenderUseScope`; the `senderIdentityId` field, with "sender identity" added to the field scope of `support.reply.propose` and `support.reply.send`; `governance.mail_identity.revise` (D01, R02, journey J13); and the refusals `sender_inactive` and `sender_not_usable`. T11 builds the list, the allocator and the adapter map, and T7 the "Send from" picker and the Owner's settings. The deletion handlers of the record and its registry digest land in the PR that first writes them (ADR-0031 item 4).

## Security invariants touched

None is weakened.
- **Tenancy:** the tenant comes only from the envelope token, and mail to the reply subdomain is rejected before any lookup (`lab:mail-inbound`); `sender_identities` is FORCE RLS, and a job's sender must belong to its workspace (`lab:tenant-conformance`, `lab:mail-send`).
- **The intersection, re-checked at dispatch and before every replay,** with the sender's status and use scope, which only narrow: `lab:mail-send`.
- **Digest-bound approval of customer messages:** nine fields, the sender identity among them; a changed sender, From, Reply-To or Bcc is a conflict (`lab:mail-send`).
- **Reconcile before retry:** `lab:mail-send`'s outcome table.
- **Broker-only credentials; fail closed:** `verify:boundaries`, `edge:check`.
- **Checks in code, never model output:** no tool takes a sender (`tool-catalogue.test.mjs`); the allocator's name folding, fallback, reserved names and collisions among live addresses (`sender-identities.test.mjs`, new).
- **`human_only` kill switches:** unchanged; the mail stop is a system stop (`stops.test.mjs`).
- **No agent effect before G10; no real mail before the `customer_mail` path (G9):** `edge:check`.
- **Immutable migrations:** `migration-history.test.mjs`.

## Consequences

- New provider resources: two Resend accounts, domain verification, DNS records and two Email Routing rules per environment (inbound, and item 10(g)'s rejecting rule with its `postmaster@` and `abuse@` forwards), each an owner action or approved resource (G6, G7).
- Residual risks: the read key could change the Resend account if the broker were compromised; a spoofed From on a domain without DMARC passes, but the reply goes only to that address after approval; merchants share the reply subdomain's sending reputation, which stays apart from Morrow's own mail and notifications; a deleted shop's address can be issued again to a later shop of the same name, but the reply subdomain takes no mail (item 10(g)), so nothing sent to the old shop reaches the new one. Blocking reuse would keep a digest of the address after the shop's data is deleted, a retention change that needs the owner.
- In a team, changing a reply's sender is an edit, so another person approves it (ADR-0024 items 7 and 8). Linking a merchant's mailbox or domain needs its own ADR: credential custody or DNS verification, and an adapter that meets items 4 to 6.

## Alternatives considered

- **Gmail OAuth:** reads whole mailboxes, and a send through it needs its own guard against a second send. Later, as an item 10 kind by its own ADR.
- **The bare domain (`<handle>@<domain>`):** one merchant's bounces and complaints would reach Morrow's own mail, and the reply key could send as Morrow.
- **Choosing the sender at approval without an edit:** the approval would bind a value outside the proposal's digest.
- **Mail accepted at the Morrow address:** a public address would start paid runs past the merchant's own mail filtering; item 1's token address stays the only way in.
- **Postmark:** no idempotency key, so a lost reply cannot be closed by a replay.
- **Automatic resend after the key window:** a duplicate to a customer is the harm prevented here.

## Owner questions

1. **Sending identity.** *Recommendation:* Morrow's reply subdomain with the shop's name and the merchant as Reply-To at launch; the merchant's own domain as a fast follow. *Changed by the owner (OD-2026-10-23-7):* a list of sending addresses per workspace, with the shop's Morrow address on the reply subdomain linked and selected by default unless the shop links its own address, and the merchant as its Reply-To, and a person picking the sender for each email job; the merchant's own mailboxes and domain are designed for, not built, and each needs its own ADR (item 10).
2. **Domain, accounts and region (G7).** *Recommendation:* one Morrow domain on Cloudflare DNS, bought in W1; separate Resend accounts for staging and production; Resend's US region; the DPA signed before real mail. ADR-0031 cites this answer.
3. **The full-access read key.** *Recommendation:* accept it as a separate broker secret limited in code to fixed GET paths.
4. **The sender check.** *Recommendation:* Email Routing plus item 3 at launch; DKIM verification in the Worker after launch.
5. **Caps.** *Recommendation:* accept item 9's numbers as configuration.
